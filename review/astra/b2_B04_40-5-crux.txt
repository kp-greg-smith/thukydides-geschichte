Thucydides 2.40.5, transmitted (end of Pericles' funeral oration): "καὶ μόνοι οὐ τοῦ ξυμφέροντος μᾶλλον λογισμῷ ἢ τῆς ἐλευθερίας τῷ πιστῷ ἀδεῶς τινὰ ὠφελοῦμεν."

Three printed renderings:
(A) English: "And we alone do good to a man fearlessly — not by a reckoning of profit more than of freedom — but on trust." (both genitives construed with λογισμῷ; τῷ πιστῷ taken by itself as "on trust")

1. **τῷ πιστῷ most naturally belongs with τῆς ἐλευθερίας**, forming a parallel to τοῦ ξυμφέροντος … λογισμῷ: calculation of advantage versus confidence associated with freedom.

2. A cautious rendering is **“with the confidence of freedom.”** (i) is an idiomatic interpretation; (ii) preserves the connection but also interprets the genitive. (iii) is syntactically much less natural, and its “but” has no explicit Greek counterpart.

3. **“That freedom gives” is explanatory expansion**, treating freedom as the source of confidence. It is an acceptable gloss, though not required by the wording. “Trust in freedom” is also interpretive—it makes freedom the object of trust—and should not be preferred merely as supposedly more literal.
