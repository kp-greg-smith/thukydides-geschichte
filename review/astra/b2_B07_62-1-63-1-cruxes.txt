----- ANTWORT/ANSWER (gpt-6-astra, effort=high; ~16.3k tokens; Anfrage 28) -----

1. **Neither.** Both verbs are **aorist subjunctives**, expressing apprehension connected with ὑποπτευόμενον: “lest the toil prove great and we prevail none the more.” Here μή introduces the feared possibility; it does **not** mean “may it not become great.”

2. The subject is **πόνος**, the toil of war.

3. **None of A–C** correctly renders this. Print something like: “As for the hardships of war—your fear that they may be great without bringing victory—let my earlier demonstrations that this apprehension is unfounded suffice you…”

4. **ἢ μηδέ means “or else … not either.”** The sense is: “Do not shirk the hardships—or else forgo seeking the honours too.” B captures this; A incorrectly coordinates two prohibitions.

5. These are **outcomes at stake**, understood as dangers to avert. B makes that implication explicit and is defensible. A’s literal “contending over … slavery … loss of rule” need not imply seeking those outcomes; calling them a “positive pair” misreads the construction.

6. **ἀπήχθεσθε means “you incurred hatred / made yourselves hated,”** an aorist middle of ἀπεχθάνομαι, not a present passive state. The relative clause concerns the grounds of that hatred; ὧν is not an ordinary direct object. **A is closest.** B captures incurred hostility, but “while acquiring rule” should be “while exercising rule.” C does not adequately express the verb’s meaning.
