Thucydides 2.8.1, transmitted Oxford/Jones text: "ὀλίγον τε ἐπενόουν οὐδὲν ἀμφότεροι, ἀλλ' ἔρρωντο ἐς τὸν πόλεμον οὐκ ἀπεικότως".
English draft A (printed): "The designs of the two sides were nothing small, and they pressed eagerly into the war not unreasonably."
Hebrew draft B (printed): "דבר קטן לא נזנח מעיני שני הצדדים, אלא מיהרו אל המלחמה" — literally "no small thing was neglected from the eyes of the two sides, rather they hastened to the war".
Hebrew draft C (proposed replacement): "דבר קטן לא תכננו שני הצדדים, אלא מיהרו אל המלחמה" — literally "nothing small did the two sides plan, rather they hastened to the war".
Question: is the transmitted sense of ἐπενόουν here "they were planning/contriving" (so that the litotes says their designs were not small — draft A and draft C), or "they had in view / took care of" (so that the sentence says they overlooked no small thing — draft B)? Which drafts are correct? Answer briefly with lexical support.

**A and C are correct on the disputed clause; B is not.** LSJ, s.v. **ἐπινοέω I.2**, gives “have in one’s mind, intend, purpose” and explicitly cites **ὀλίγον οὐδέν, Th. 2.8**. ([LSJ](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn%3Acite2%3Ascaife-viewer%3Adictionaries.v1%3Alsj-n40331/))

Thus “both sides were planning nothing small” expresses ambitious designs by litotes. “Have in view” is appropriate only in the sense of *intend*: it does not support “overlooked no small thing.” B introduces neglect, which the Greek does not express, and changes the force of the negative.

C therefore fixes the disputed meaning, though as a translation of the whole quotation it still omits **οὐκ ἀπεικότως**, “not unreasonably.”
