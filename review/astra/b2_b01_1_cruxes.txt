(2) 2.6.2 Context: Athenians instruct the Plataeans, who hold Theban prisoners, before Athens deliberates. Greek: "ἐς τὴν Πλάταιαν ἔπεμψαν κήρυκα, κελεύοντες εἰπεῖν μηδὲν νεώτερον ποιεῖν περὶ τῶν ἀνδρῶν οὓς ἔχουσι Θηβαίων, πρὶν ἄν τι καὶ αὐτοὶ βουλεύσωσι περὶ αὐτῶν"
English draft: "to do nothing new about the Thebans whom they held, before the Athenians themselves should have deliberated."
Question: literal "new" or idiomatic "nothing rash/irreversible"? Which should a faithful modern translation print?

(3) 2.5.5 Context: Plataean herald's message to the Thebans. Greek: "λέγοντες ὅτι οὔτε τὰ πεποιημένα ὅσια δράσειαν ἐν σπονδαῖς σφῶν πειράσαντες καταλαβεῖν τὴν πόλιν"

----- ANTWORT/ANSWER (gpt-6-astra) -----

1. **2.3.1 — Idiomatic; no text problem.** νεωτερίζειν here means to take disruptive or hostile measures against someone. “Doing nothing new” preserves the etymology but obscures the force. Print: **“especially since the Thebans were taking no hostile action against anyone.”** “Hostile” is more precise here than “rash”; LSJ explicitly places this passage under the sense “use forcible measures.” [LSJ, νεωτερίζω I.2](https://translate.academic.ru/νεωτερίζειν%20ἐβούλετο%20ἐς%20τὸ%20πλῆθος/el/xx/)

2. **2.6.2 — Idiomatic; no text problem.** μηδὲν νεώτερον ποιεῖν means to refrain from a drastic or rash step affecting the prisoners. Print: **“to take no rash action concerning the Theban prisoners until the Athenians themselves had deliberated about them.”** “Nothing irreversible” conveys the practical implication, but *irreversibility* is contextual, not the adjective’s lexical meaning. Bare “nothing new” is misleading in modern English.

3. **2.5.5 — A defensible transmitted construction; no emendation required.** δράσειαν is the **third-person plural aorist optative of δράω**, reporting an aorist indicative in indirect speech after the historical governing verb. Its subject is the Thebans; τὰ πεποιημένα is its object, with ὅσια qualifying the deeds predicatively: approximately, “they had not acted righteously in doing what they had done.” πειράσαντες specifies the offending action. Your draft therefore gives the sense correctly: **“what they had done was impious—attempting to seize their city while a truce was in force.”** The compressed wording does not itself justify a textual-uncertainty note; it can stand unannotated.

4. **2.8.4 — Draft A’s temporal sense is standard; no text problem.** κεκωλῦσθαι presents a resulting state, “be blocked/held up,” viewed prospectively with future παρέσται. It does not require “had previously been hindered.” The ancient scholia likewise paraphrase it as an undertaking that *will be hindered unless the individual comes*. [Scholia on 2.8.4](https://eulogikon.org/works/thucydides-comm-scholia-uku-aa)

   One correction to A: **ἐν τούτῳ … ᾧ is correlative**, “in that undertaking/respect in which,” rather than “in this eagerness.” Print: **“Each thought that any undertaking in which he himself did not take part would be held up.”**

5. **2.10.2 — “Two-thirds”; conventional fractional expression, no text problem.** Greek can omit the denominator when it exceeds the numerator by one: here, two parts out of three. Print **“two-thirds of each city’s force assembled at the appointed time at the Isthmus.”** “Two contingents” loses the fraction expressed by the idiom. [Smyth, §1125a](https://grammars.alpheios.net/smyth/xhtml/body.1_div1.4_div2.16.html)
