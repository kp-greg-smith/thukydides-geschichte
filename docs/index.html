Translation policy

Translate directly from Ancient Greek, faithfully and readably rather than word for word. Other translations, including Braun, Hobbes and Crawley, may help with understanding but are not the source.

  1. Use consistent equivalents by meaning, not mechanically by word. In particular, αἰτία may express accusation or causation.
  2. Add nothing and do not exaggerate.
  3. Do not soften the force of the Greek.
  4. Add brief notes for genuine textual or interpretive uncertainty and significant changes in a key term’s sense.
  5. Preserve ambiguity where the Greek permits multiple readings.
  6. Take particular care with speeches and indirect speech, preserving viewpoint, argument, negation, conditions, modality and time.

Work in batches of at most ten chapters, with Greek alongside each section. Check section counts, length ratios and potentially unfinished sentences automatically, then review every finding against the Greek.
